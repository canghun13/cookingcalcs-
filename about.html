About CookingCalcs

CookingCalcs is a free collection of cooking calculators, reference charts and step-by-step guides for home cooks, bakers, culinary students and small food businesses. It covers the arithmetic of the kitchen — how much, how long, how hot and how much it costs — and every tool shows the formula and the figures it uses, so you can check the answer instead of just trusting it.

Why We Built This

Cooking involves a surprising amount of math. How many grams is a cup of flour? How many medium eggs replace three large ones? How long does a 14-pound turkey take? What should a dish cost on a menu? These questions come up constantly, and the answers are usually buried in a long article or need a calculator and a conversion chart side by side.

CookingCalcs puts the tool and the explanation on the same page. Type in what you have, get the answer, and see how it was worked out.

What You'll Find Here

  • Calculators and converters — cups to grams by ingredient, liquid and weight units, oven temperatures, egg sizes, butter, can sizes, pan sizes and more. Volume-to-weight conversions use a different density for each ingredient, so a cup of flour and a cup of sugar give different answers.
  • Cooking times and temperatures — USDA safe internal temperatures, cooking times by weight and method, grilling, deep frying, reheating and timing a whole meal so it finishes together.
  • Scaling and cooking for a crowd — recipe multipliers, catering pan servings, pot sizes and batch cooking.
  • Costs and kitchen math — cost per serving, meal and grocery budgets, and worked word problems with printable worksheets for culinary and hospitality courses: unit price, yield percentage, food cost, labor cost, menu pricing and more.
  • Guides — long-form references that pull each topic together in one place, from baking conversions to vintage recipe measurements.

Where the Numbers Come From

Safe cooking temperatures and egg weights follow the USDA (Food Safety and Inspection Service guidance and the US egg grading standard). Ingredient weights come from USDA FoodData Central and standard culinary references. Cooking times start from USDA charts and university extension publications, adjusted for method and size. Kitchen math pages follow the formulas taught in open culinary math textbooks.

We review the tools and data at least once a year, and sooner when official guidance changes or a reader points out an error. Cooking is never a purely precise science, so times and temperatures are starting points — a thermometer and your own judgment always win.

The main references behind the site, if you want to check them yourself:

  • USDA FSIS Safe Minimum Internal Temperature Chart — meat, poultry, seafood and egg dish temperatures
  • USDA FoodData Central — ingredient weights and portion sizes
  • USDA AMS United States Standards for Shell Eggs — egg size weight classes
  • Culinary Math (Open Washington open textbook) — yield, costing and recipe conversion formulas
