How to Turn Cookie Dough Into Bars: Pan Size, Temperature and Bake Time

November 2026 · 6 min read

Press the dough ¾ to 1 inch thick, bake at 350°F, and allow about 2½ times the cookie bake time. A typical chocolate chip recipe (about 7 cups of dough) fills a 9×13 pan and bakes in roughly 25–33 minutes. Pick the pan from the dough volume: 4–6 cups for an 8- or 9-inch square, 6–8 cups for a 9×13, more than 8 for a half sheet.

Baking drop-cookie dough as one pan of bars saves forty minutes of scooping and rotating trays, which is why it is the standard trick for bake sales and cookies for a crowd. The method below follows King Arthur Baking's guide to turning a cookie recipe into bars, with the arithmetic worked through.

Step 1: Estimate How Much Dough You Have

Add up the cups of the main ingredients and ignore anything under 3 tablespoons (salt, baking soda, vanilla). For a classic chocolate chip recipe:

IngredientAmountCounts as
Flour2¼ cups2¼ cups
Sugars (white + brown)¾ + ¾ cup1½ cups
Butter1 cup (2 sticks)1 cup
Eggs2 largeabout ⅓ cup
Chocolate chips2 cups2 cups
Totalabout 7 cups

The creamed dough ends up a little smaller than the raw total, but the estimate is close enough to choose a pan.

Step 2: Choose the Pan

DoughPanBars when cut
4–6 cups8" or 9" square16 (4 × 4)
6–8 cups9×13"24 (4 × 6) or 32 (4 × 8)
more than 8 cups13×18" half sheet48 (6 × 8)

The goal is a layer ¾ to 1 inch thick; thicker bars stay chewier in the middle, thinner ones bake crisper. Line the pan with a parchment sling — two strips crossing, with the ends hanging over the sides — so the whole slab lifts out for clean cutting.

Step 3: Temperature and Time

  • Oven: 350°F. If the cookie recipe says 375°F or higher, lower it to 350°F so the edges don't overbake before the center sets.
  • Time: multiply the cookie bake time by 2½ — or by 3 for a smaller pan with dough thicker than ¾ inch.
  • If you lowered the temperature, add 2–5 minutes for every 25°F.

Worked example: the chocolate chip recipe above bakes 9–11 minutes as cookies at 375°F. As bars: 9–11 × 2½ = 23–28 minutes, plus 2–5 minutes for dropping to 350°F, so about 25–33 minutes in a 9×13 at 350°F. Start checking at 25.

Step 4: Tell When They're Done

Look for edges that have puffed slightly and turned golden, and a center that looks set rather than glossy and wet. A toothpick in the middle should come out with a few moist crumbs, not clean — bars keep cooking from the pan's heat and firm up as they cool. Let them cool completely, at least an hour, before cutting; warm bars tear and crumble.

Which Cookies Work as Bars

  • Work well: chocolate chip, oatmeal raisin, peanut butter, M&M, white chocolate macadamia, snickerdoodle (sprinkle the cinnamon sugar on top), most drop cookies.
  • Don't: thin crisp cookies such as lace cookies or tuiles, meringue cookies, and cookies that depend on a shape, such as cut-out sugar cookies or sandwich cookies.

Bars freeze well for up to 3 months, wrapped tightly once cooled. For other pan swaps — a 9×13 recipe in two square pans, say — the baking pan size converter gives the scaling factor, and the cake to cupcake converter handles cake batter.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you bake cookie dough as bars?

Yes. Most drop cookie doughs — chocolate chip, oatmeal, peanut butter, M&M — press into a pan and bake as bars. Use a pan that leaves the dough ¾ to 1 inch thick, bake at 350°F, and allow about 2½ times the cookie bake time.

How long do you bake cookie bars in a 9x13 pan?

About 2½ times the cookie time at 350°F. A recipe that bakes 9–11 minutes as cookies at 375°F takes about 25–33 minutes as bars in a 9×13 at 350°F.

What size pan for cookie bars?

Add up the cups of the main ingredients. About 4–6 cups of dough fits an 8- or 9-inch square pan, 6–8 cups a 9×13, and more than 8 cups a 13×18-inch half sheet pan.

How do you know when cookie bars are done?

The edges puff slightly and turn golden, the center looks set rather than wet, and a toothpick in the middle comes out with a few moist crumbs. They firm up as they cool, so don't wait for a clean toothpick.
