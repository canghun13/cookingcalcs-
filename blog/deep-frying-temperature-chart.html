Deep Frying Temperatures and Times by Food

September 2026 · 7 min read

Deep frying looks like it should have one setting. It doesn't — the useful range runs from about 325°F to 375°F, and where a food sits in that range depends on how thick it is and whether the inside needs cooking or just warming.

The times below assume a properly sized batch. If the pot is crowded the oil drops out of range and every one of these numbers stops applying, which is covered in the batch calculator.

Potatoes and Vegetables

FoodOil tempTimeDone when
French fries — first fry325°F4-6 minSoft, pale, no colour yet
French fries — second fry375°F2-3 minGolden and rigid
Potato chips / crisps350°F3-4 minBubbling slows, edges curl
Onion rings350°F2-3 minGolden, batter set
Battered vegetables350°F2-4 minBatter crisp and pale gold
Tempura vegetables340-350°F1-2 minPale, lacy, barely coloured

Chicken and Meat

FoodOil tempTimeDone at
Fried chicken — bone-in325-350°F12-16 min165°F internal
Chicken tenders / strips350°F4-6 min165°F internal
Chicken wings375°F8-10 min165°F internal
Chicken nuggets (frozen)350°F4-5 min165°F internal
Schnitzel / thin cutlets350°F3-4 minGolden, 165°F for poultry
Corn dogs350°F3-4 minDeep gold, batter firm

Bone-in chicken is the one item where the thermometer isn't optional. It's thick enough that the crust reaches its finished colour well before the centre reaches 165°F — see the meat temperature guide for the full set of safe temperatures.

Seafood, Doughnuts and Sides

FoodOil tempTimeDone when
Battered fish fillets350-360°F4-6 minBatter deep gold, fish flakes
Shrimp / prawns350°F2-3 minOpaque and curled
Calamari360-375°F1-2 minPale gold — longer turns it rubbery
Doughnuts375°F1-2 min per sideGolden ring around the middle
Churros360-375°F2-4 minDeep gold, ridges crisp
Spring rolls / egg rolls350°F3-5 minWrapper blistered and gold

The Three Temperature Bands

Sixteen individual numbers is more than anyone remembers. Three bands is manageable, and the band a food belongs to follows from one question: does the inside need cooking, or just heating?

BandFoodsWhat it's for
325-340°FBone-in chicken, first-fry potatoes, tempuraThe interior needs real cooking time, so the crust has to form slowly enough to wait for it
350°FMost things — tenders, fish, shrimp, onion rings, spring rolls, nuggetsThe default. Fast enough to crisp, gentle enough not to burn
360-375°FDoughnuts, churros, wings, calamari, second-fry potatoesThe inside is already cooked or cooks instantly — this is purely about the crust

When you meet a food that isn't on any chart, ask that question and you'll land in the right band. Thick and raw goes low; thin, pre-cooked, or delicate goes high.

Why Some Foods Are Fried Twice

Double-frying looks like fuss and isn't. A thick-cut chip has two incompatible requirements: the potato inside must cook through, which takes time at moderate heat, and the outside must crisp, which takes high heat quickly. Try to do both in one pass and you get a raw centre or a burnt shell.

Splitting it solves the conflict. The first fry at 325°F cooks the potato and drives off surface moisture without colouring it. The food can then rest — even for an hour — before the second fry at 375°F builds the crust in two or three minutes. Restaurant fries are crisp for exactly this reason, and the rest between fries is part of the method rather than a convenience.

Reading Doneness Without a Thermometer

CueWhat it means
Vigorous bubbling slowsSurface moisture has cooked off — the crust is forming
Item rises and floatsCommon for battered foods; usually near done
Colour deepens past goldPast done for most things; pull it
Batter still soft when pressedNeeds longer, or the oil is too cool

These cues work for battered and thin items. They do not work for bone-in chicken, where the crust finishes long before the interior — that one needs 165°F confirmed on a thermometer regardless of how it looks.

Adjusting for Altitude

Frying is one of the methods elevation actually changes. Moisture escapes the food faster in thinner air, so at a standard temperature the outside browns before the inside is ready. Lower the oil by roughly 3°F per 1,000 feet — at 5,000 feet, a 375°F target becomes about 360°F. The altitude cooking calculator covers this alongside the boiling and pressure adjustments.

Frequently Asked Questions

What temperature should I deep fry at?

Most deep frying happens between 325°F and 375°F, and 350°F is the standard starting point for anything unfamiliar. Lower temperatures suit thick items that need the inside to cook through; higher temperatures suit thin items and anything that only needs a fast crust.

Why are french fries fried twice?

The first fry at a lower temperature, around 325°F, cooks the potato through without browning it. The second at 375°F crisps the outside in a couple of minutes. Doing it in one pass means either a raw centre or a burnt exterior, because the two goals need different temperatures.

How do I know when fried food is done without a thermometer?

Colour and float are the usual cues — most battered items rise and the bubbling slows noticeably as the surface moisture cooks off. For chicken and anything thick, use a thermometer anyway: poultry needs 165°F internal, and colour is not a reliable indicator of that.

Can I deep fry frozen food without thawing?

Yes, and you generally should — thawing leaves the surface wetter, which makes the spattering worse. Shake off loose ice first, lower the food in rather than dropping it, use a smaller batch, and expect the oil to take longer to recover.

How many times can I reuse frying oil?

Judge it by condition rather than a count. Strain the oil once it cools, keep it in a sealed container away from light, and discard it when it darkens noticeably, smells rancid, foams on the surface, or starts smoking below its usual frying temperature. Battered and breaded foods shorten its life fastest because they shed particles.
