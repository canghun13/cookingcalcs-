How Long to Cook Chicken Drumsticks — Times by Oven Temperature

July 2026 · 6 min read

Drumsticks are the most forgiving cut of chicken there is. They're dark meat with a bone running through the thickest part, which means they take longer than a boneless piece but tolerate a much wider doneness window — there's no narrow window to hit the way there is with a breast.

Cook Time by Oven Temperature

Oven TempTimeSkin Result
375°F40-50 minCooked through, skin soft
400°F35-45 minGolden, lightly crisp
425°F30-40 minCrisp and browned — best all-round
450°F25-35 minVery crisp, watch closely
425°F, from frozen45-55 minCooked, but skin stays soft

Flip once halfway through at any temperature. Larger drumsticks sit at the upper end of each range — size varies a lot more between packages than most people expect, which is the main reason two people following the same recipe get different results.

Dark Meat: Why 165°F Is Just the Floor

165°F is the USDA safe minimum for poultry, and drumsticks reach it well before they're at their best. Drumsticks are worked leg muscle with noticeably more connective tissue than breast meat, and that tissue doesn't soften until higher temperatures. Pulled at exactly 165°F, the meat closest to the bone is often still chewy.

Internal TempResult
165°F (74°C)Safe to eat — but chewy near the bone
175-185°F (79-85°C)Connective tissue softening, meat pulls easily
185-195°F (85-91°C)Very tender, falls off the bone

The reason this works for drumsticks but not for chicken breast comes down to fat content. Dark meat has enough intramuscular fat to stay moist well past 165°F; a lean breast doesn't, which is why it genuinely does dry out if pushed the same way. See the Meat Temperature Guide for the full comparison.

Drumsticks vs Thighs vs Wings

All three are dark meat and share the same generous doneness window, but their geometry sets the timing:

CutTime at 400°FWhy
Boneless thighs20-25 minFlat and thin, no bone to slow heat
Bone-in thighs35-45 minBone and thickness add time
Drumsticks35-45 minBone runs through the thickest part
Wings40-50 minSmall, but time is spent rendering skin and fat

Wings take longer than their size suggests because the goal there isn't just cooking through — it's rendering skin. Full detail in How Long to Cook Chicken Thighs and How Long to Cook Chicken Wings.

Getting Crisp Skin

Dry the skin. Pat thoroughly with paper towels before seasoning. Surface moisture steams the skin rather than crisping it.

Bake at 400°F or higher. Lower temperatures cook the meat fine but leave the skin pale and soft.

Use a wire rack, or flip. Drumsticks sitting in their own rendered fat won't crisp underneath. A rack solves it; flipping halfway is the fallback.

Sauce last. Most barbecue sauces contain sugar and will burn over a 40-minute bake. Brush it on in the final 5-10 minutes, or after the drumsticks come out.

Grill and Air Fryer

On a grill, drumsticks do best over indirect medium heat for 30-35 minutes, moved over direct heat for the last few minutes to char the skin — see Direct vs Indirect Heat and the Grill Temperature & Time Calculator. In an air fryer, expect about 22-28 minutes at 400°F, turning once.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do you bake chicken drumsticks at 400°F?

About 35-45 minutes, flipping once halfway through. Larger drumsticks sit at the upper end of that range. At 425°F they take roughly 30-40 minutes, and at 375°F closer to 40-50 minutes.

Do drumsticks and thighs cook in the same time?

Not quite. Boneless thighs are flat and thin, so they cook fastest — about 20-25 minutes at 400°F. Bone-in thighs take 35-45 minutes, and drumsticks take a similar 35-45 minutes because the bone runs through the thickest part. All three are dark meat and share the same generous doneness window.

Why is 165°F not the best target for drumsticks?

165°F is the safe minimum, but drumsticks have more connective tissue than breast meat, and it doesn't break down until higher temperatures. Pulled at exactly 165°F the meat tends to be chewy near the bone. Most cooks prefer 175-195°F, where the connective tissue softens — and because dark meat is fattier, it stays juicy in that range instead of drying out.

How do I get crispy skin on baked drumsticks?

Pat them completely dry before seasoning, bake at 400°F or higher, use a wire rack so air circulates underneath, and leave space between pieces so they roast instead of steam. A few minutes under the broiler at the end adds extra crispness. Sauce should go on in the last 5-10 minutes, not at the start, since sugar in it burns.
