How Long to Cook Chicken Wings — Times by Oven Temperature

July 2026 · 6 min read

Wing cook time is really a question about skin, not safety. Wings hit the safe 165°F long before they're actually good — the extra time is what renders the fat and crisps the skin, which is the whole point of a wing.

Cook Time by Oven Temperature

Oven TempWhole WingsDrumettes / FlatsSkin Result
350°F55-70 min50-60 minCooked through, skin soft
400°F40-50 min35-45 minLightly crisp, juicy
425°F35-45 min30-40 minCrisp and golden — best all-round
450°F30-35 min25-30 minVery crisp, watch for burning
425°F, from frozen50-60 min45-55 minCooked, but less crisp

Flip the wings halfway through at any temperature. 425°F is the most reliable single setting: hot enough to render fat and brown the skin, not so hot that the outside burns before the inside finishes.

Why 165°F Is the Wrong Target for Wings

165°F is the USDA safe minimum for all poultry, and wings are no exception — but safe and good aren't the same number here. Wings are full of skin, fat, and connective tissue, and at exactly 165°F none of that has rendered yet: the skin is rubbery and the meat clings tightly to the bone.

Internal TempResult
165°F (74°C)Safe to eat — but rubbery skin, chewy texture
175-185°F (79-85°C)Fat rendered, skin crisp, meat pulls easily

This is the same principle behind ribs and brisket needing far more than their safe minimum. Unlike a lean chicken breast — which genuinely does dry out past 165°F — wings have enough fat that the higher range doesn't hurt them. Compare the full chart in our Meat Temperature Guide.

The Baking Powder Trick

Tossing wings with a small amount of baking powder before baking is the single most effective way to get crisp skin in a home oven. It raises the skin's surface pH, which accelerates browning and helps pull moisture out of the skin. Use about ½ to 1 teaspoon per pound, mixed into your seasoning, and make sure it's baking powder, not baking soda — soda is much stronger and leaves a soapy, metallic taste.

Three Things That Matter More Than Time

Pat them completely dry. Surface moisture steams the skin instead of crisping it. Paper towels before seasoning; if you have time, leave them uncovered in the fridge for a few hours to dry further.

Use a wire rack. Wings sitting directly on a pan cook in their own rendered fat, so the underside never crisps. A rack lets air circulate underneath.

Don't crowd the pan. Wings packed together trap steam between them. Leave visible space around each one, and use two pans rather than one crowded one.

Sauce at the End, Never Before

Sauce applied before baking prevents the skin from crisping and often burns — most wing sauces contain sugar. Bake the wings dry, then toss in sauce in a bowl immediately after they come out, while they're still hot.

Grill and Air Fryer

On a grill, wings take about 15-20 minutes over direct medium heat (around 350-400°F), turning regularly — see the Grill Temperature & Time Calculator for zone setup. In an air fryer, expect roughly 22-26 minutes at 400°F, shaking the basket halfway; the higher airflow crisps skin faster than a conventional oven.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do you bake chicken wings at 400°F?

About 40-50 minutes for whole wings, flipping halfway, or 35-45 minutes for separated drumettes and flats. At 425°F the same wings take roughly 35-45 minutes; at 350°F they need 55-70 minutes but the skin ends up noticeably softer.

Should chicken wings be cooked past 165°F?

Yes, for texture. 165°F is the safe minimum, but wings are full of connective tissue and fat that need more time and heat to render — pulling them at exactly 165°F usually gives rubbery skin and chewy meat. Most cooks find 175-185°F produces noticeably better wings, and unlike a lean breast, wings don't dry out at that range.

Why do recipes add baking powder to chicken wings?

Baking powder raises the skin's surface pH, which speeds up browning reactions and helps draw out moisture, producing crisper skin than heat alone. Use aluminum-free baking powder — not baking soda, which is far stronger and leaves a metallic taste. About ½ to 1 teaspoon per pound of wings, tossed with the seasoning, is typical.

How long do frozen chicken wings take to bake?

Roughly 50-60 minutes at 425°F, about 50% longer than thawed wings. They also won't crisp as well, since the ice releases moisture into the oven — patting them dry partway through the cook helps. Thawing first and drying thoroughly always gives better skin.
