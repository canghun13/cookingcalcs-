How Long to Cook Pork Tenderloin — Times by Weight and Oven Temperature

July 2026 · 7 min read

Pork tenderloin recipes online disagree wildly — some say 8 minutes, some say over an hour. Almost all of that gap traces back to one of two things: whether the recipe was actually written for tenderloin or for the much larger pork loin, and whether it seared the meat first.

Cook Time by Weight and Oven Temperature (No Sear)

Weight350°F375°F400°F425°F
0.75 lb22-27 min19-23 min15-19 min12-16 min
1 lb27-35 min24-29 min20-25 min17-22 min
1.5 lb35-42 min30-37 min26-32 min23-28 min
2 lb42-50 min37-44 min32-38 min28-34 min

Searing First? Oven Time Roughly Cuts in Half

Searing the tenderloin on the stovetop first — 2-3 minutes per side in a hot pan — brings the exterior most of the way to done before it ever sees the oven. That means far less oven time is needed to finish the center:

WeightSear TimeOven Time at 400°F (After Searing)
0.75-1 lb2-3 min per side8-12 min
1.5-2 lb3-4 min per side12-16 min

This is the single biggest reason two recipes for the same size tenderloin land on wildly different total times — an unsealed recipe (20-25 minutes) and a seared one (8-12 minutes) are both correct for what they describe, they're just describing different processes.

Wrapped in Foil vs. Open

Wrapping the tenderloin in foil traps steam and cooks it more gently, taking 25-30 minutes at 400°F — but the surface won't brown. Left uncovered, it cooks faster and develops color, at the tradeoff of slightly more moisture loss if overcooked. Foil is worth it if the tenderloin is topped with something delicate that would burn exposed; otherwise, open roasting gives a better crust.

The Real Cause of Most Timing Problems: Tenderloin vs. Loin

This is the single most common source of the "it was still raw after 25 minutes" complaints that show up in recipe comments. Pork tenderloin and pork loin are two entirely different cuts that share a confusingly similar name, and swapping one recipe's time onto the other's meat guarantees a bad result.

TenderloinLoin
Typical weight0.75-1.5 lb2-5 lb
ShapeLong, narrow, taperedWide, thick roast
Thickness~2-3 inches~4-5 inches
Cook time (400°F)20-25 min (per pound above)50-70 min total, regardless of the tenderloin chart
Often sold asTwo to a packageSingle large roast

If what's on the cutting board is noticeably wide and heavy for a piece this short, it's a loin — treat it as its own roast, not a large tenderloin, and expect roughly double to triple the time.

Target Temperature and the Carryover Trick

145°F (63°C), rested for at least 3 minutes, is the USDA target for pork tenderloin. Because tenderloin has almost no connective tissue or fat to render — unlike brisket or ribs, which need to go well past their safe minimum to become tender — cooking it exactly to 145°F in the oven and then resting risks slightly overshooting into dry territory. Pulling it at 140-142°F and letting carryover heat finish the rise during the rest gives a juicier result more reliably.

Why This Cut Breaks the Cluster's Usual Rule

Most of the cuts on this site — brisket, ribs, chicken wings and drumsticks — need to be cooked well past their food-safety minimum because they're full of connective tissue that only turns tender with extended time above that minimum. Pork tenderloin is the opposite case: it's lean, with essentially no connective tissue to break down, so there's nothing to gain by cooking past 145°F — only moisture to lose. It's the exception that proves the rule: the "cook past minimum for texture" logic only applies to cuts that actually have collagen to render.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do pork tenderloin recipes give such different cook times?

Mostly because "pork tenderloin" and "pork loin" get confused, and they're very different sizes — a tenderloin is a thin 0.75-1.5 lb muscle, while a loin is a thick 2-5 lb roast. Cooking a loin for tenderloin time leaves it raw in the center. The rest of the gap comes from whether the recipe sears first, and whether it's wrapped in foil.

How do I tell pork tenderloin and pork loin apart?

Tenderloin is long, narrow, and tapered — about 2-3 inches thick and usually 0.75-1.5 lb, often sold two to a package. Loin is a thicker, wider roast, typically 2-5 lb and roughly 4-5 inches thick. If what you're holding is noticeably wide and heavy for its length, it's a loin, not a tenderloin, and needs much longer to cook.

What internal temperature should pork tenderloin be cooked to?

145°F (63°C), then rested for 3 minutes. Because tenderloin is lean with almost no connective tissue or fat to render, pulling it a few degrees early — around 140-142°F — and letting carryover heat finish the rise during rest gives a juicier result than waiting for the thermometer to hit 145°F in the oven.

Does searing pork tenderloin before baking make a big difference in time?

Yes — searing first cuts oven time roughly in half. A seared tenderloin only needs about 8-12 minutes in the oven to finish, since the stovetop sear has already brought the exterior most of the way there. Without searing, the same tenderloin needs 20-25 minutes in the oven to reach the same result.
