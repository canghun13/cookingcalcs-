How Many Cups in a Stick of Butter?

August 2026 · 6 min read

1 stick of butter = ½ cup = 8 tablespoons = 4 oz = 113g. Two sticks make 1 cup. Four sticks make 1 pound.

That single line answers most butter questions. The rest of this page is for the cases where it doesn't — going the other direction, and the two common amounts that a stick wrapper stubbornly refuses to divide into.

Cups to Sticks

Recipes written in cups are just as common, and this is the direction people search for almost as often. The rule is simply the reverse: multiply cups by two to get sticks.

The two bolded rows are the awkward ones. Everything else lands on a whole number of tablespoons, which means you can cut it straight off the wrapper.

The Two Amounts the Wrapper Can't Give You

A US stick is printed with eight tablespoon marks, so any amount that works out to whole tablespoons is a knife cut away. ⅓ cup and ⅔ cup are the exceptions, and they turn up constantly in cookie and quick-bread recipes.

You needExactlyWhat to actually do
⅓ cup5⅓ tbspCut at the 5-tbsp mark, then add 1 teaspoon. Or weigh 76g
⅔ cup10⅔ tbspCut a full stick plus 2 tbsp and 2 teaspoons. Or weigh 151g

Why a teaspoon works: a tablespoon is three teaspoons, so the leftover ⅓ tablespoon in ⅓ cup is exactly one teaspoon, and the ⅔ tablespoon in ⅔ cup is exactly two. Nothing is being rounded here — the teaspoon closes the gap precisely.

Some brands print a ⅓-cup line on the wrapper alongside the tablespoon marks, which saves the arithmetic when it's there. It often isn't, and it's worth checking rather than assuming, because misreading a ⅓-cup line as a tablespoon mark is a five-fold error.

A 250g European Block Is Not Two Sticks

Two US sticks weigh 227g. A European or UK block is usually 250g, which is about 10% more — closer to 2¼ sticks. It looks like the same product in a similar shape, which is exactly why the substitution slips through.

For cookies or a cake batter, 10% of the butter is a difference you probably won't notice. For shortcrust or laminated pastry, where the fat-to-flour ratio is doing structural work, it changes the result. Weigh it when the recipe is pastry. Our complete butter conversion guide covers the other origin mismatches, including old recipes that call for a knob or a lump.

Measure Before You Melt

When a recipe says "½ cup melted butter," measure the stick first and melt it afterwards. Pouring melted butter into a measuring cup is the less accurate route: foam sits on top, a film stays behind in the pan, and reading the meniscus in a cup of opaque liquid is guesswork.

The weight doesn't change when butter melts, so if you have a scale the question disappears entirely — 113g is 113g in either state. The order only matters when you're measuring by volume.

Salted or Unsalted Doesn't Change the Conversion

Salted and unsalted butter are identical in weight and volume, so every number on this page applies to both. What changes is the seasoning: if you use salted butter in a recipe written for unsalted, reduce the added salt by roughly ¼ teaspoon per stick. Brand doesn't affect the conversion either, since the US stick is a packaging standard rather than a manufacturer's choice.

Whipped butter is the real exception. It has air beaten into it, so a cup of whipped butter weighs noticeably less than 227g and shouldn't be substituted by volume in baking. Full detail on measuring technique is in how to measure butter.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many cups are in a stick of butter?

One US stick of butter is half a cup. That is also 8 tablespoons, 4 ounces, or about 113 grams. Two sticks make a full cup, and four sticks make a pound.

How many sticks of butter is 1 cup?

Two sticks. Going the other direction, divide the number of sticks by two to get cups and multiply cups by two to get sticks — so ½ cup is 1 stick, ¾ cup is 1½ sticks, and 2 cups is 4 sticks.

How many tablespoons are in a stick of butter?

8 tablespoons. The wrapper on a US stick is printed with tablespoon marks, so you can cut what you need without measuring — each printed line is one tablespoon.

How do I measure ⅓ cup of butter from a stick?

⅓ cup is 5⅓ tablespoons, which is the one common amount the wrapper marks cannot give you exactly. Cut at the 5-tablespoon line and add one teaspoon, or weigh 76 grams. ⅔ cup has the same problem at 10⅔ tablespoons — cut at 10 and add two teaspoons.

Is a European 250g block the same as two sticks?

No. Two US sticks are 227g, so a 250g block is about 10% more — roughly 2¼ sticks. Treating the block as two sticks leaves you short, which matters in pastry where the fat-to-flour ratio is doing structural work.

Should I measure butter before or after melting?

Before, whenever you can. Measuring a solid stick by its wrapper marks is more reliable than pouring melted butter into a measuring cup, where foam, residue left in the pan, and the meniscus all cost you a little. If a recipe says "½ cup melted butter," measure one stick and then melt it.
