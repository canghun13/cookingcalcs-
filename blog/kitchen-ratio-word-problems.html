Kitchen Ratio Word Problems: Solving Recipe Ratios Step by Step

November 2026 · 12 min read

Add the parts, divide, multiply. A 3:1 vinaigrette has 4 parts. For 2 cups, one part is 2 ÷ 4 = ½ cup, so the oil is 3 × ½ = 1½ cups and the vinegar ½ cup. If you know one ingredient instead of the total, divide that ingredient by its own number of parts to find one part.

A kitchen ratio describes a recipe by proportion instead of fixed amounts, so it can be made in any size. Culinary math courses use them to teach proportion, and cooks use them because a ratio is easier to remember than a recipe. The ratios below are the standard teaching versions; real recipes adjust them, but the math is the same. When you know the ratio of every ingredient to the flour, the problem becomes baker's percentage.

The Formulas

Total parts = add every number in the ratio
One part = total amount ÷ total parts
Each ingredient = its parts × one part
Working from one ingredient: one part = that amount ÷ its parts

Level 1: Two-Part Ratios

A vinaigrette is 3 parts oil to 1 part vinegar. How much of each do you need for 3 cups of dressing?

Total parts = 3 + 1 = 4. One part = 3 ÷ 4 = ¾ cup.

Oil = 3 × ¾ = 2¼ cups. Vinegar = ¾ cup. Check: 2¼ + ¾ = 3 cups.

Level 2: Three or More Parts

Mirepoix is 2 parts onion, 1 part carrot and 1 part celery by weight. How much of each goes into 6 lb of mirepoix?

Total parts = 2 + 1 + 1 = 4. One part = 6 ÷ 4 = 1.5 lb.

Onion = 2 × 1.5 = 3 lb. Carrot = 1.5 lb. Celery = 1.5 lb.

The common slip is dividing by 3 because there are three vegetables. Divide by the number of parts, not the number of ingredients.

Level 3: Working From One Ingredient

Pie dough is 3 parts flour, 2 parts fat and 1 part water by weight. You have 450 g of flour. How much fat and water do you need, and how much dough will you make?

Flour is 3 parts, so one part = 450 ÷ 3 = 150 g.

Fat = 2 × 150 = 300 g. Water = 150 g. Dough = 450 + 300 + 150 = 900 g.

When the problem gives one ingredient instead of the total, divide by that ingredient's parts, not the total parts.

Level 4: Ratios as Percentages

Pound cake is 1:1:1:1 — equal weights of flour, butter, sugar and eggs. How much of each for 2 kg of batter, and what percentage of the batter is each ingredient?

Total parts = 4. One part = 2,000 ÷ 4 = 500 g of each.

Each ingredient is 1 ÷ 4 = 25% of the batter.

Write the 3:2:1 pie dough as baker's percentages, where flour is 100%.

Divide every part by the flour's 3: flour 3 ÷ 3 = 100%, fat 2 ÷ 3 = 66.7%, water 1 ÷ 3 = 33.3%.

A ratio and a baker's percentage describe the same recipe; the baker's percentage just sets the flour to 100 instead of to its number of parts. The baker's percentage calculator does the conversion for any formula.

Level 5: By Weight or by Volume?

A 1:1 simple syrup is made with 1 cup of sugar and 1 cup of water. Is it really 1:1?

By volume, yes. By weight, no: a cup of granulated sugar weighs about 200 g and a cup of water about 237 g, so the weight ratio is 200:237, or about 0.85:1.

That is why pastry and bread ratios are almost always given by weight, while dressings, drinks and rice are usually given by volume. Use whichever unit the ratio was written in; switching midway changes the recipe. The cups to grams converter handles the conversion when you have to.

Rice is cooked at 1 part rice to 2 parts water by volume. How much water do you need for 2½ cups of rice?

Here the problem gives one ingredient, so one part = 2½ cups. Water = 2 × 2½ = 5 cups.

Level 6: Sizing a Ratio From Portions

You are dressing 40 salads with 1½ fl oz of 3:1 vinaigrette each. How much oil and vinegar do you need?

Total dressing = 40 × 1.5 = 60 fl oz. One part = 60 ÷ 4 = 15 fl oz.

Oil = 3 × 15 = 45 fl oz (5⅝ cups). Vinegar = 15 fl oz (1⅞ cups).

Find the total from the portions first, then split it by the ratio. Doing it the other way round — scaling a recipe — is covered in recipe conversion factor word problems.

Common Mistakes

  • Dividing by the number of ingredients. A 2:1:1 mirepoix has three vegetables but four parts.
  • Using total parts when you know one ingredient. If the flour is 450 g and flour is 3 parts, one part is 450 ÷ 3, not 450 ÷ 6.
  • Mixing weight and volume. A ratio written by weight does not work in cups, and the reverse.
  • Skipping the check. The ingredient amounts must add back to the total.

Kitchen Ratio Word Problems — Answer Key

  1. 3 cups. One part = 1½ cups. Water = 2 × 1½ = 3 cups.
  2. 1½ cups oil, ½ cup vinegar. 4 parts. One part = 2 ÷ 4 = ½ cup. Oil 3 × ½ = 1½ cups.
  3. Onion 5 lb, carrot 2.5 lb, celery 2.5 lb. 4 parts. One part = 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5 lb.
  4. 600 g flour, 400 g fat, 200 g water. 6 parts. One part = 1,200 ÷ 6 = 200 g.
  5. 1 lb each; 4 lb batter. Butter is 1 part, so one part = 1 lb. 4 parts × 1 lb = 4 lb.
  6. Sugar 2.5 lb, butter 5 lb, flour 7.5 lb. 6 parts. One part = 15 ÷ 6 = 2.5 lb.
  7. Sugar 2 cups, lemon juice 4 cups, water 12 cups. 9 parts. One part = 18 ÷ 9 = 2 cups.
  8. Rice 1 cup, water 2½ cups. 7 parts. One part = 3½ ÷ 7 = ½ cup. Rice 2 × ½ = 1 cup; water 5 × ½ = 2½ cups.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you solve a ratio word problem in cooking?

Add the numbers in the ratio to get the total parts, divide the total amount by the total parts to find one part, then multiply each ingredient's parts by that. For 3 cups of a 3:1 vinaigrette: 4 parts, one part is ¾ cup, so 2¼ cups oil and ¾ cup vinegar.

Are recipe ratios by weight or by volume?

It depends on the ratio. Baking ratios such as 3-2-1 pie dough and 1:1:1:1 pound cake are by weight. Dressings, drinks and rice are usually by volume. Use the unit the ratio was written in.

What is the 3-2-1 pie dough ratio?

3 parts flour, 2 parts fat and 1 part water by weight. With 450 g of flour, that is 300 g of fat and 150 g of water, for 900 g of dough.

What do you do when you only know one ingredient?

Divide that ingredient's amount by its own number of parts to find one part. If 450 g of flour is 3 parts, one part is 150 g, and every other ingredient follows from that.
