Measurement Conversion Word Problems

Updated August 2026 · 6 min read

Recipe measurement conversions come up constantly in real cooking — a broken scale, a missing measuring spoon, a recipe written in a unit you don't have on hand. Below are worked examples covering the conversions that trip people up most, step by step.

Worked Example Problems

A recipe calls for ¾ cup of butter, but you only have a tablespoon measure. How many tablespoons do you need?
1 cup = 16 tablespoons, so ¾ cup = ¾ × 16 = 12 tablespoons.

You need 2¼ teaspoons of vanilla extract, but your smallest spoon is a tablespoon. How much of a tablespoon is that?
1 tablespoon = 3 teaspoons, so 2¼ teaspoons ÷ 3 = ¾ of a tablespoon (0.75 tbsp).

A recipe calls for 350g of all-purpose flour. Your scale is broken — how many cups is that?
All-purpose flour is about 120g per cup, so 350 ÷ 120 = about 2⅞ cups (roughly 2¾-3 cups, since flour measurement varies by how it's scooped).

You have a 500ml bottle of milk and a recipe needs 2 cups. Do you have enough?
1 cup = 237ml, so 2 cups = 474ml. Since 500ml > 474ml, yes, you have enough, with about 26ml left over.

A recipe calls for 1 lb of butter, but your sticks are labeled in tablespoons (1 stick = 8 tbsp = ½ cup). How many sticks do you need?
1 lb of butter = 2 cups = 4 sticks (since ½ cup per stick × 4 = 2 cups). You need 4 sticks.

You need to scale down 3 tablespoons of oil to teaspoons for a small-batch recipe.
1 tablespoon = 3 teaspoons, so 3 tablespoons × 3 = 9 teaspoons.

A soup recipe makes 6 cups, and you want to freeze it in 1-pint containers (1 pint = 2 cups). How many containers do you need?
6 cups ÷ 2 cups per pint = 3 containers.

A roast recipe calls for 2.5 lbs of meat, but your kitchen scale only reads in ounces. What do you set it to?
1 lb = 16 oz, so 2.5 × 16 = 40 ounces.

A recipe from a UK cookbook says to preheat the oven to 180°C. Your oven only shows Fahrenheit — what do you set it to?
°F = (°C × 9/5) + 32, so (180 × 1.8) + 32 = 324 + 32 = 356°F (close enough to round to the standard 350°F setting).

A European recipe calls for 1.5 liters of stock, but your measuring cups are in US cups. How many cups do you need?
1 liter = about 4.2 cups, so 1.5 × 4.2 = about 6.3 cups (roughly 6¼ cups).

Common Mistakes When Converting Measurements

Mixing up the tablespoon-to-teaspoon ratio. It's 1 tablespoon = 3 teaspoons, not 2 — this is the single most common measurement conversion error in home cooking.

Using one weight-to-volume conversion for every ingredient. A cup of flour, a cup of sugar, and a cup of honey all weigh different amounts, because volume measures space and weight measures mass — density differs by ingredient. See the Cups to Grams Converter for ingredient-specific figures rather than guessing with one number for everything.

Confusing fluid ounces with weight ounces. A fluid ounce measures volume; a regular ounce measures weight. They're only interchangeable for water — for anything else, converting a recipe by treating "oz" as always meaning the same thing will throw off the result.

Rounding too early in a multi-step conversion. If a problem requires converting grams to cups and then scaling the recipe, round only at the very end — rounding partway through compounds the error by the time you reach the final answer.

Trying to hit an exact temperature conversion instead of the nearest standard setting. Converting 180°C gives 356°F, but most home ovens are only dialed in 25°F increments — set it to 350°F rather than hunting for a dial position between 350 and 375 that doesn't exist.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many tablespoons are in a cup?

16 tablespoons. To convert cups to tablespoons, multiply the number of cups by 16. To go the other way, divide the tablespoon count by 16.

Why can't I use one gram-to-cup conversion for every ingredient?

Because a cup measures volume, and different ingredients pack differently into that same volume — a cup of flour weighs much less than a cup of sugar, which weighs less than a cup of honey. Each ingredient needs its own gram-to-cup figure; there's no universal conversion number that works for all of them.

Is a fluid ounce the same as a regular ounce?

No — a fluid ounce measures volume, while a regular ounce measures weight. They happen to be equal only for water (1 fl oz of water weighs almost exactly 1 oz), but for anything else — flour, sugar, oil — 1 fluid ounce and 1 ounce by weight are different amounts.

What's the formula for converting Celsius to Fahrenheit?

°F = (°C × 9/5) + 32. So 180°C becomes (180 × 1.8) + 32 = 356°F. For oven temperatures specifically, most home ovens only have marked settings every 25°F, so round to the nearest standard setting (350°F, 375°F, 400°F) rather than trying to dial in an exact degree — see the Oven Temperature Converter for the full chart including Gas Mark.
