Old UK Egg Sizes (Size 1 to 7): What to Use in an Old Recipe Today

November 2026 · 6 min read

An old recipe's size 3 egg (60–65 g) is today's UK large, or a US extra-large. The numbered sizes ran from size 0 (largest) to size 7 (smallest) in 5-gram steps and were replaced by Small, Medium, Large and Very Large in 1996. Size 1 and 2 eggs are the largest UK large or very large eggs (US jumbo); size 4 is a UK medium (US large).

British cookbooks and magazine recipes from the 1970s to the mid-1990s call for eggs by number: "3 size 3 eggs", "2 size 2 eggs". Those numbers don't appear on boxes any more, and the new names don't line up one-to-one with the old numbers, which is why the question still comes up whenever someone bakes from a family recipe or a secondhand book.

The weight bands for sizes 0–7 come from the EU egg marketing rules then in force (Regulation 1274/91, Article 8), which the UK followed. Today's UK bands are Small under 53 g, Medium 53–63 g, Large 63–73 g and Very Large 73 g and over. US sizes are the USDA weight classes for a single egg.

A Worked Example: The Classic Sponge

A typical old all-in-one sponge recipe asks for 4 size 3 eggs with 8 oz each of self-raising flour, butter and caster sugar. Four size 3 eggs weigh about 4 × 62.5 = 250 g in the shell, or roughly 220 g once cracked.

  • In the UK: 4 large eggs (about 270 g in the shell) is a touch generous; 4 medium (about 230 g) is a touch short. Either works in a sponge — or use 3 large and 1 medium.
  • In the US: 4 extra-large eggs (256 g) is the match. With US large eggs (57 g), use 4 and add a tablespoon of beaten egg from a fifth.

Many old sponge recipes also use the weigh-the-eggs method: weigh the eggs in their shells and use the same weight of flour, butter and sugar. If yours does, egg size stops mattering at all — the recipe adjusts to whatever eggs you have.

When the Difference Matters

One size up or down is about 5 g per egg, which a cake or batter absorbs without anyone noticing in recipes with one or two eggs. It starts to matter in recipes built on eggs — sponges, custards, soufflés, meringues and enriched doughs — and in anything using five or more. For those, total the weight using the calculator above and match it as closely as you can; a kitchen scale beats any size label. Frequently Asked Questions

What is a size 3 egg today?

A size 3 egg weighed 60–65 g in the shell. The British Egg Information Service recommends using a large egg (63–73 g) where an old recipe says size 3. In US sizes, the closest match is extra-large (about 64 g).

What size is a size 1 egg?

Size 1 eggs weighed 70–75 g in the shell — the top of today's UK large band or the bottom of very large. In the US, a jumbo egg (about 71 g) is the closest match.

When did UK egg sizes change from numbers to small, medium and large?

The numbered sizes 0–7 were replaced by Small, Medium, Large and Very Large on 1 August 1996, with packers given until 30 June 1997 to switch over.

Is a UK large egg the same as a US large egg?

No. A UK large egg weighs 63–73 g in the shell; a US large egg is about 57 g. A UK large is closer to a US extra-large or jumbo, and a UK medium (53–63 g) is closer to a US large.
