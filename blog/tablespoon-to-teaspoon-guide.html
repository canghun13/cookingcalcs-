How Many Teaspoons in a Tablespoon?

Updated October 2026 · 9 min read

1 tablespoon = 3 teaspoons. That holds for US, UK, Canadian and metric recipes alike. Australia is the one exception, where a tablespoon is 4 teaspoons. Use the converter for any amount, or the charts below for every common fraction.

The Key Relationships

  • 1 tablespoon = 3 teaspoons
  • 1 cup = 16 tablespoons = 48 teaspoons
  • ½ cup = 8 tablespoons, ¼ cup = 4 tablespoons
  • 1 tablespoon = ½ fluid ounce ≈ 15 ml; 1 teaspoon ≈ 5 ml

Tablespoon or Teaspoon? Reading the Abbreviations

Tablespoon is written tbsp, Tbsp, tbs, Tb or a capital T. Teaspoon is tsp or a lowercase t. In handwritten and older recipes the case is the only clue — capital T is the tablespoon — so read it carefully: mixing the two means three times too much or a third too little.

On a measuring spoon set the tablespoon is always the largest spoon, then 1 tsp, ½ tsp and ¼ tsp. A British dessert spoon sits in between: 2 teaspoons, about 10 ml. It turns up in older UK cookbooks but almost never in American recipes.

Level, Heaping, and Spoons From the Drawer

Recipe measurements are level unless the recipe says otherwise. Fill the spoon and sweep the top flat with the back of a knife; "heaping" means the ingredient mounds above the rim. For liquids, fill to the brim — a slight dome from surface tension is normal.

Cutlery isn't made to a cooking standard. A soup spoon from the drawer can hold anywhere from about 8 to 18 ml, so it is fine for stirring tea and unreliable for baking soda. Measuring spoons are cheap and remove that whole source of error.

The same spoons work for dry and liquid ingredients; the dry-versus-liquid distinction only matters for cups. For honey or syrup, a light film of oil or cooking spray on the spoon lets it slide out cleanly.

When You Don't Have the Right Spoon

  • 1 tablespoon = the teaspoon three times
  • ½ tablespoon = 1½ teaspoons; ⅓ tablespoon = 1 teaspoon
  • ¾ teaspoon = ½ tsp + ¼ tsp
  • ½ teaspoon without the spoon: fill a teaspoon, level it, and push half off with a knife
  • ⅛ teaspoon ≈ a small pinch between thumb and forefinger

When Precision Really Matters

The 3× ratio is large compared with the amounts involved, so a spoon mix-up hurts more than a cup mix-up. A tablespoon of baking soda instead of a teaspoon makes baked goods taste soapy and metallic; a tablespoon of salt or cayenne can make a dish inedible. Oil, mild herbs and garlic are forgiving — save the careful levelling for leavening, salt and strong spices.

Is a Tablespoon the Same Everywhere?

Nearly. The US tablespoon is 14.79 ml and the UK and Canadian tablespoons are treated as 15 ml — the same for practical purposes. Australia uses a 20 ml tablespoon, equal to four teaspoons. Following an Australian baking recipe with US spoons, multiply the tablespoon count by about 1.33, or use 1 tbsp + 1 tsp per Australian tablespoon. Savory cooking rarely notices; leavening does.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many teaspoons in 2 tablespoons?

2 tablespoons equals 6 teaspoons. The ratio is always 3 teaspoons per tablespoon, so multiply the tablespoons by 3.

How many tablespoons in ⅓ cup?

5 tablespoons plus 1 teaspoon. A cup is 16 tablespoons, which doesn't divide evenly by 3, so the extra teaspoon makes up the difference.

How many ml is a tablespoon?

A US tablespoon is 14.79 ml, rounded to 15 ml in recipes. A teaspoon is 4.93 ml, rounded to 5 ml. An Australian tablespoon is 20 ml.

Is a capital T tablespoon or teaspoon?

Capital T is tablespoon; lowercase t is teaspoon. It matters most in handwritten and older recipes, where the abbreviation is the only clue.

Is a dessert spoon the same as a tablespoon?

No. A dessert spoon is a British measure of 2 teaspoons, about 10 ml — between a teaspoon and a tablespoon.

Does "1 tbsp" mean level or heaping?

Level, unless the recipe says heaping or rounded. Fill the spoon and sweep the top flat with a straight edge.
