Turkey Breast vs Chicken Breast: Cooking Time & Temperature Differences

July 2026 · 6 min read

Turkey breast and chicken breast are the same kind of meat, cooked to the exact same safe temperature — and yet nobody roasts them the same way. A chicken breast goes into a hot 400°F oven for well under half an hour. A turkey breast goes into a gentle 325°F oven for an hour or more. The reason isn't the meat itself, it's the size.

Same Target, Different Approach

Chicken BreastTurkey Breast
USDA safe internal temp165°F (74°C)165°F (74°C)
Typical weight6-8 oz3-8+ lb
Recommended oven temp400°F (high, fast)325°F (low, slow)
Doneness rangeNone — must hit 165°F exactlyNone — must hit 165°F exactly

Neither cut gets any margin on temperature — poultry doesn't have a "medium-rare" option the way beef or lamb does, so both need to reach 165°F throughout with no exceptions. The difference is entirely about mass: a chicken breast is small enough that a hot oven cooks it through before the outside overcooks, while a turkey breast is large enough that the same hot oven would burn the surface long before heat reaches the center.

Cook Time Comparison

Chicken Breast (8 oz, 400°F)Turkey Breast (per pound, 325°F)
Boneless22–26 min total16–20 min per pound
Bone-in35–45 min total~20 min per pound

These aren't directly comparable minute-for-minute since turkey breast is scaled per pound and chicken breast is a fixed small size, but the pattern holds: chicken breast finishes as a single quick roast, while turkey breast is planned by weight because the range of sizes is so much wider. For the full breakdown by weight, see the turkey breast cooking time guide and chicken breast cooking time guide.

Why the Oven Temperature Differs

Heat has to travel from the outside of a piece of meat to its center, and the distance it travels scales with size — a chicken breast is thin enough that even a hot 400°F oven cooks the center through in 20-something minutes without over-browning the surface first. A turkey breast is often three to ten times the mass, meaning heat needs far more time to reach the middle. At 400°F, the outside of a turkey breast would brown, then dry out, then risk burning, long before the center caught up. Dropping to 325°F slows the whole process down, giving the inside time to reach 165°F while the outside browns at a manageable pace.

Can You Use the Same Method for Both?

Not really, at least not without adjustment. Cooking a turkey breast at chicken-breast temperatures (400°F+) will cut the time by roughly 30%, but risks a burnt or overly dark exterior with an undercooked center — the classic "looks done, isn't done" turkey problem. Going the other direction — roasting a small chicken breast at turkey-breast temperatures (325°F) — mostly just wastes time and can leave it slightly pale rather than browned, since the lower heat isn't hot enough to develop good color on something that small and thin.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do turkey breast and chicken breast cook to the same temperature?

Yes — both are poultry, so both need to reach 165°F (74°C) at the thickest part, per USDA guidelines. There's no temperature difference between them, unlike some other protein comparisons. The difference is entirely in how you get there.

Why does chicken breast use a hotter oven than turkey breast?

Size. A chicken breast is small and thin enough that a hot oven (400°F) browns the outside and cooks it through in under 30 minutes without drying it out. A whole turkey breast is many times larger — the same 400°F would char the outside long before the thick center reached 165°F, so a lower, slower 325°F lets heat penetrate evenly before the surface overcooks.

Can I cook a turkey breast at 400°F like a chicken breast to save time?

You can, and it cuts roughly 30% off the time, but it comes with real risk — the outside can brown too far, and even burn, before the thick interior reaches a safe temperature. If you do cook turkey breast hot, cover it loosely with foil partway through to slow surface browning while the inside catches up, and check the center with a thermometer well before the estimated time is up.

Which dries out more easily, chicken breast or turkey breast?

Chicken breast, proportionally — its small size and lack of a forgiving doneness range (poultry must hit 165°F throughout) mean there's very little margin between done and overcooked, and that margin shrinks further at high oven temperatures. Turkey breast has a larger overall mass, so it takes longer to overcook once it reaches temperature, though a poorly timed roast can still dry out significantly.
