Turkey Breast vs Whole Chicken: Which Cooks Faster?

July 2026 · 7 min read

For a smaller holiday meal, a turkey breast and a whole chicken are the two obvious choices — but they don't cook the way their size would suggest. A turkey breast, despite being a compact, boneless roast, often takes longer per pound than an entire chicken with bones, cavity, and all. Here's why, with times side by side.

The Surprising Part: Whole Chicken Is Often Faster

Turkey Breast (bone-in)Whole Chicken
Conventional oven temp325°F (low and slow)375-425°F (hot, for crisp skin)
Safe internal temp165°F165°F (breast and thigh)
4-5 lb roast time1 hr 15 min - 1 hr 40 min60-75 min

At the exact same weight, whole chicken typically finishes sooner. That's not because a whole bird is somehow simpler to cook through — it's almost entirely about oven temperature convention. Turkey breast recipes default to 325°F because that's the safe, gentle temperature a whole turkey needs so its legs and breast finish around the same time. A breast roasted alone doesn't have that problem, but most recipes keep the low-and-slow habit anyway. Whole chicken recipes, by contrast, commonly run hot (375-425°F) specifically to render fat and crisp the skin — and that heat cooks the meat faster as a side effect.

Cook Time by Weight, Side by Side

WeightTurkey Breast (bone-in, 325°F)Whole Chicken (375°F)
2-3 lb50-65 min35-50 min
3-4 lb—45-60 min
4-5 lb1 hr 15 min - 1 hr 40 min60-75 min
5-6 lb—75-90 min
6-7 lb1 hr 50 min - 2 hr 10 min90-105 min (est.)

Whole chickens are rarely sold much larger than 7-8 lb, so the comparison naturally stops around there — for anything bigger, you're back to choosing between turkey breast and a full turkey. Whole-chicken times above assume unstuffed, room-temperature-start roasting and vary by oven accuracy and whether the bird is trussed; always confirm with a meat thermometer rather than time alone. For the full turkey breast weight range including boneless times, see our turkey breast cooking time guide.

Want Turkey Faster? Turn Up the Oven

If the schedule matters more than tradition, there's no food-safety reason a turkey breast has to be roasted at 325°F. That temperature is a whole-turkey habit, not a breast-specific requirement. Roasting a turkey breast alone at 350-375°F — the same range a whole chicken uses — brings its time much closer to a chicken's, typically shaving 15-25% off the times in most 325°F charts. Start checking internal temperature earlier than the low-temp chart suggests, since the meat will reach 165°F sooner.

Servings and Cost: Where Chicken Pulls Ahead

Whole chicken has more inedible weight per pound than turkey breast — bones, cavity, and a higher skin-to-meat ratio mean you need roughly 3/4 to 1 lb of raw chicken per person, versus about 1/2 lb boneless (or 3/4 lb bone-in) turkey breast per person. So a chicken and a turkey breast of the same raw weight won't feed quite the same number of people; the chicken feeds slightly fewer.

Cost usually flips that math back in chicken's favor. Whole chicken commonly runs under $2/lb in the US, while turkey breast — especially boneless — is often $4-6/lb. Even after accounting for chicken's lower edible yield, it typically comes out cheaper per serving. Which Should You Choose?

If you specifically want turkey for the holiday and don't mind a slightly longer wait, a bone-in turkey breast at its conventional 325°F is the simplest, most forgiving option — it's hard to overcook and needs little attention. If time or budget is the deciding factor, a whole chicken roasted hot wins on both counts, and it's a familiar, low-risk swap for anyone who's roasted a chicken before but never tackled turkey.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a turkey breast cook faster than a whole chicken?

Usually not — at the same weight, a whole chicken often finishes faster. Whole chicken is conventionally roasted hot (375-425°F) for crisp skin, while turkey breast is conventionally roasted low and slow (325°F), a habit carried over from whole-turkey roasting even though a breast alone doesn't need it. The oven temperature convention, not the shape of the meat, is usually what decides which finishes first.

Can I roast a turkey breast at a higher temperature to save time?

Yes. A boneless or bone-in turkey breast doesn't need the gentle 325°F treatment a whole turkey gets — that lower temperature exists mainly to keep a large bird's legs and breast finishing together. Roasting a turkey breast alone at 350-375°F works fine and closes most of the time gap with a whole chicken; just start checking temperature earlier since it will finish faster than the 325°F times listed on most charts.

How many people does a whole chicken feed compared to a turkey breast?

Plan on roughly 3/4 to 1 lb of raw whole chicken per person, versus about 1/2 lb boneless or 3/4 lb bone-in turkey breast per person — whole chicken has more inedible weight (bone, skin, cavity) per pound, so a same-sized bird feeds slightly fewer people than a turkey breast roast.

Which is cheaper, whole chicken or turkey breast?

Whole chicken, usually by a wide margin — it commonly runs under $2/lb in the US, while turkey breast (especially boneless) is often $4-6/lb. For a budget-conscious small gathering, whole chicken typically wins on cost per serving even after accounting for its lower edible yield per pound.
