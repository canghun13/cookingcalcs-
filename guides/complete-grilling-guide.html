The Complete Grilling Guide

Updated August 2026 · 6 min read

CookingCalcs has one interactive grill calculator and three grilling explainers, each answering a specific piece of the same question: how hot, which zone, and for how long. This page ties them together — the tool to look up any food fast, and the background on heat zones, fuel types, and no-thermometer temperature checks that make the numbers make sense.

Start Here: Look Up Any Food

The Grill Temperature Calculator covers 14 common foods — burgers, steaks, chicken, pork, sausage, salmon, shrimp, ribs, and vegetables — with the recommended heat zone, temperature, hand-test count, estimated time, and safe internal temperature for each, plus a full reference chart if you'd rather scan than select.

The Two Zones, in Short

Direct HeatIndirect Heat
Food positionRight over the flameBeside the flame, lid closed
Typical temp400-550°F+250-350°F
Use forBurgers, steaks, chops, fish, shrimp, thin vegetablesWhole chicken, ribs, roasts, brisket

See Direct vs Indirect Heat for the full explanation, including how to set up both zones on either grill type and what goes wrong if you use the wrong one.

Charcoal or Gas — Does It Change the Numbers?

No — the temperatures, zones, and hand-test counts on this site apply the same way to charcoal and gas. What differs is how fast each reaches temperature and how precisely you can hold a number: gas is dial-controlled and repeatable, charcoal runs hotter at its peak but takes longer to light and is adjusted through vents and coal placement. See Charcoal vs Gas Grill: Temperature Differences for the full comparison.

No Thermometer? Use Your Hand

SecondsHeat LevelApprox. Temp
2-3 secHigh450-550°F+
4-5 secMedium-High400-450°F
6-7 secMedium350-400°F
8-10 secMedium-Low300-350°F
10-12+ secLow250-300°F

Hold your palm 3-4 inches above the grate and count how long you can hold it there. It's a quick estimate, not a precision reading — reliable for telling high from medium from low, less so for a multi-hour low-and-slow cook where a small drift matters. Full detail, including common testing mistakes, in How to Check Grill Temperature Without a Thermometer.

Which Fuel Should You Actually Buy?

The temperatures and zones on this page work identically on charcoal and gas, so fuel choice is really about how you like to cook, not about a different set of numbers to learn. Lump charcoal lights fastest and generally reaches the highest peak temperatures, making it the pick for quick, hot searing sessions. Briquettes burn longer and steadier, better suited to multi-hour indirect cooks like ribs or a whole chicken. Gas trades some peak-heat ceiling for speed and repeatability — dial to a number and it holds there, no vent-and-coal management required. See Charcoal vs Gas Grill: Temperature Differences for the full breakdown, including how to read a gas grill's BTU rating.

A Simple Decision Path for Any New Cook

QuestionAnswer → Action
Does it finish in under 20-25 minutes and is it relatively thin?Yes → direct heat. No → indirect heat, or sear-then-move for thick single cuts.
No built-in or reliable grill thermometer?Use the hand test — 3-4 inches above the grate, count seconds.
Cooking two things with very different cook times together?Start the slower item first, add the faster one partway through so both finish together.
Unsure if it's done?Check the safe internal temperature — visual cues alone aren't reliable for ground meat or poultry.

Running through these four questions covers the large majority of what actually goes wrong for new grillers — using the wrong zone, guessing at heat instead of testing it, timing multiple items badly, and trusting color instead of a thermometer.

Safety Doesn't Change on the Grill

The safe internal temperatures are identical to oven cooking — 165°F for poultry, 145°F for pork and whole-muscle beef/lamb, 160°F for ground meat — the grill is a heat source, not a different safety standard. See the Meat Temperature Guide for the full chart, and the Cooking Time Calculator if you'd rather work from weight than a food lookup.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where do I start if I've never grilled before?

Start with the hand test to learn how to judge heat without a thermometer, then use the Grill Temperature Calculator to look up your food before you cook. Once you're comfortable with direct heat for quick items like burgers and chicken breast, move on to indirect heat for larger items like whole chicken or ribs.

Do I need different techniques for charcoal versus gas?

The core techniques — direct vs indirect zones, the hand test, target temperatures — are identical on both. The practical difference is speed and control: charcoal takes longer to light and is adjusted with vents and coal placement, while gas responds instantly to the dial.

What's the single biggest grilling mistake beginners make?

Using direct heat for everything. It works fine for quick, thin foods like burgers and chicken breast, but a whole chicken, ribs, or a thick roast will burn on the outside long before the center is safe if left over direct heat the whole time — those need indirect heat, or a sear-then-move approach for thick single cuts like steak.
