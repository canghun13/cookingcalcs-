How to Use This Calculator

Select the type of meat, choose the cut, enter the weight, and select your cooking method. The calculator returns an estimated cooking time and the safe internal temperature to verify with a thermometer.

Each result shows the oven temperature it assumes and the minutes per pound behind it. Times assume meat straight from the fridge; a large roast that has sat out for 30–60 minutes will finish a little sooner. Gammon follows the UK rule of thumb — 20 minutes per pound plus 20 when boiled, 30 per pound plus 30 when roasted — and a boiled joint ignores the method setting.

Why the Cooking Method Changes the Time

The base times above assume conventional oven roasting. Grilling runs hotter than most ovens, so direct-heat grilling typically finishes about 10-15% faster than the same cut in the oven — this calculator adjusts for that automatically when you select Grill/BBQ. Pan/stovetop cooking applies direct, concentrated heat to both sides, which is faster still, but only makes sense for thinner cuts (steaks, chops, patties, fillets) — a whole roast, brisket, or leg of lamb isn't practical to pan-cook, and the calculator will flag that if you select a large cut with the Pan/Stovetop method.

Why You Still Need a Thermometer

Cooking times are estimates. Actual time varies based on your oven's accuracy, the shape of the cut, whether it was at room temperature, and how full your oven is. The only reliable way to know meat is safe is to check the internal temperature with an instant-read thermometer. Times give you a starting point; temperature tells you when it's done.

Pork Cooking Times by Cut

A pork loin roast takes about 20 minutes per pound at 350°F to reach 145°F, followed by a 3-minute rest. Pork shoulder for pulled pork is a different job: about 60–90 minutes per pound at 225–250°F, until it reaches 195–205°F and shreds. One-inch chops take 15–20 minutes at 400°F after a quick sear, whatever the pack weighs.

Ham and Gammon Times

Gammon is raw cured pork; once cooked it becomes ham. The UK rule of thumb is 20 minutes per pound (450 g) plus 20 minutes when simmered, or 30 minutes per pound plus 30 minutes roasted at 180°C (350°F). A fully cooked US ham only needs reheating to 140°F.

How Accurate Are These Times?

Each result assumes the oven temperature shown, a typically shaped cut and meat straight from the fridge. Ovens often run 15–25°F off their dial and cuts vary in shape at the same weight, so treat the time as when to start checking with a thermometer, not when to stop. For individual pieces — breasts, thighs, chops, steaks, fillets — the calculator gives a fixed range, because six chicken breasts in one layer take no longer than two. Thickness is what changes the time.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long to cook chicken breast at 400°F?

A 6–8 oz boneless breast takes about 20–26 minutes at 400°F; bone-in breasts take 35–45 minutes. Check that the thickest part reaches 165°F (74°C).

How long to cook a whole chicken per pound?

About 20 minutes per pound plus 15 minutes at 375°F, so a 4 lb chicken takes roughly 1 hour 35 minutes. The thigh should reach 165°F.

How long do you cook gammon per kg?

Simmered: about 40 minutes per kg (20 per lb) plus 20 minutes. Roasted at 180°C: about 60 minutes per kg (30 per lb) plus 30 minutes. A 2 kg joint is roughly 1 h 40 min simmered or 2 h 30 min roasted.

How long to cook a pork loin roast?

About 20 minutes per pound at 350°F, to 145°F in the center, then rest 3 minutes. A 3 lb loin takes about an hour.

How long does a 10 lb turkey breast take?

About 3 hours 20 minutes at 325°F (20 minutes per pound). Tent with foil if the skin browns before the center reaches 165°F.

Does resting time count as cooking time?

No. Rest starts once the meat leaves the heat, but the center keeps rising 3–5°F, which is why you can pull meat just below its target.

Why is the safe temperature for pork lower than it used to be?

The USDA lowered whole cuts of pork from 160°F to 145°F with a 3-minute rest in 2011, once trichinosis had become negligible in commercial pork. Older recipes still quote 160°F.

Why did my meat take longer than the calculator said?

Usually an oven running cooler than its dial, a thicker-than-average cut, or meat that went in colder than assumed. If it happens every time, check the oven with a separate oven thermometer.
