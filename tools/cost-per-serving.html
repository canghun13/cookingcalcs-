Cost Per Serving Calculator

Divide the total price by the number of servings to get cost per serving — a $12 dish that makes 4 servings costs $3.00 per serving. Enter your total price and serving count below for any recipe or packaged food; useful for meal planning and comparing homemade cost against takeout or delivery.

How to Use This Calculator

Enter the total cost of the meal or recipe and the number of servings it makes. The calculator divides the cost and compares your result against typical home cooking benchmarks — so you can tell at a glance whether a meal is budget-friendly, average, or on the expensive side.

Why Cost Per Serving Matters

A $12 rotisserie chicken sounds expensive until you realize it yields 4–5 servings, making it $2.40–3.00 per serving — cheaper than most alternatives. A $3 can of beans sounds cheap, but if a recipe only uses half the can, the cost per serving is even lower. Thinking in per-serving terms reveals the true value of ingredients.

This calculation is especially useful when comparing home cooking to takeout or delivery. A home-cooked dinner at $3.50 per serving looks completely different against a $16 delivery order for the same dish. Cost per serving puts both on the same scale.

How to Calculate Cost Per Serving

The formula is simple:

Cost per serving = Total recipe cost ÷ Number of servings

The harder part is accurately calculating the total recipe cost — especially when you're using partial packages. For example, if a recipe uses 300g of ground beef from a 500g pack that cost $6.00, the beef cost for that recipe is $6.00 × (300 ÷ 500) = $3.60, not $6.00. Worked Example Problems

These are the same style of price-per-serving word problems you'll see in a consumer math class — buying by the pound, dividing into ounce-sized servings. Here's how to work through them step by step.

You purchase cheese for $4 per pound. Each serving uses 4 ounces. How much does the cheese cost per serving?
There are 16 ounces in a pound, so the price per ounce is $4 ÷ 16 = $0.25. A 4-ounce serving costs 4 × $0.25 = $1.00 per serving.

You purchase beef at $6 per pound. Each serving uses 6 ounces. What is the cost per serving?
Price per ounce: $6 ÷ 16 = $0.375. A 6-ounce serving costs 6 × $0.375 = $2.25 per serving.

You purchase potatoes at $1.60 per pound. Each serving requires 8 ounces. What is the cost per serving?
Price per ounce: $1.60 ÷ 16 = $0.10. An 8-ounce serving (half a pound) costs 8 × $0.10 = $0.80 per serving.

You purchase butter at $3.20 per pound. Each serving requires 2 ounces. What is the cost per serving?
Price per ounce: $3.20 ÷ 16 = $0.20. A 2-ounce serving costs 2 × $0.20 = $0.40 per serving.

You purchase chicken breast for $5.60 per pound. Each serving uses 5 ounces. What is the cost per serving?
Price per ounce: $5.60 ÷ 16 = $0.35. A 5-ounce serving costs 5 × $0.35 = $1.75 per serving.

You purchase rice for $2.40 per pound. Each serving requires 3 ounces. What is the cost per serving?
Price per ounce: $2.40 ÷ 16 = $0.15. A 3-ounce serving costs 3 × $0.15 = $0.45 per serving.

You purchase salmon for $9.60 per pound. Each serving uses 6 ounces. What is the cost per serving?
Price per ounce: $9.60 ÷ 16 = $0.60. A 6-ounce serving costs 6 × $0.60 = $3.60 per serving.

You purchase ground turkey for $4.80 per pound. Each serving requires 4 ounces. What is the cost per serving?
Price per ounce: $4.80 ÷ 16 = $0.30. A 4-ounce serving costs 4 × $0.30 = $1.20 per serving.

The pattern is always the same: divide the price per pound by 16 to get the price per ounce, then multiply by the serving size in ounces. Or skip the middle step and use the calculator above — enter the total package price as "Total cost" and calculate how many of that serving size fit in a pound as your "servings" number.

Cost Per Serving: Home Cooking vs Takeout vs Delivery

The comparison that makes cost per serving useful is putting home cooking next to the alternatives. A home-cooked pasta dinner typically runs $2–4 per serving in ingredients. The same dish from a casual restaurant runs $12–18 per serving, and delivery adds another $3–6 in fees and tip on top of that. Over a week of dinners for a family of four, that gap is easily $200–400 — which is why per-serving math, not total receipt totals, is what actually shows the savings.

Restaurant and delivery pricing also bundles in things home cost-per-serving math doesn't: labor, rent, and packaging. That's a fair trade some weeks, but it means a "$15 meal" from a restaurant and a "$15 meal" you cook at home aren't really the same comparison — the home version usually makes 3–4 servings from that $15, not one.

How Buying in Bulk Changes the Number

Cost per serving drops when you buy ingredients in bulk, but only if you use what you buy before it spoils. A 5 lb bag of rice is cheaper per serving than a 1 lb bag, but only if the extra 4 lb doesn't go stale in the pantry. The same logic applies to proteins: a family pack of chicken thighs is usually 20–30% cheaper per pound than a small pack, and thighs freeze well, so the bulk discount is close to risk-free. Fresh produce is the opposite case — buying more than you'll use in a week often erases the bulk savings through waste.

Ways to Lower Your Cost Per Serving

Increase servings without increasing cost. Batch cooking is the simplest lever. A pot of chili that costs $14 and makes 4 servings is $3.50 each. The same pot scaled to 6 servings (same cost, a bit more liquid and beans) drops to $2.33 each.

Switch to cheaper proteins. Protein is usually 50–70% of a meal's cost. Chicken thighs cost significantly less than breasts. Canned tuna, eggs, lentils, and beans are all far cheaper per gram of protein than any meat.

Use whole ingredients. Pre-cut vegetables, marinated meats, and pre-made sauces all carry a significant convenience premium. Buying whole and prepping yourself typically cuts ingredient cost by 20–40%.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's a reasonable cost per serving for home cooking?

For breakfast, $0.50–2.50 is typical. For lunch, $1–5. For dinner, $1.50–6. Anything under $3 per serving for a full dinner is very good. Above $6–7 per serving for home cooking suggests you could reduce cost with different proteins or bulk ingredients.

Should I include cooking oil and spices in the total cost?

For a rough estimate, no — these add fractions of a cent per serving. For a precise budget breakdown, yes — enter the full package cost and estimate the portion used. Even precise tracking usually adds less than $0.15 per serving for common pantry staples.

How is this different from the Meal Cost Calculator?

This tool is for quick calculations — total cost divided by servings. The Meal Cost Calculator lets you build up cost ingredient by ingredient, which is more accurate but takes more time. Use this one for packaged foods and simple meals, and the Meal Cost Calculator when you're cooking from scratch.

How do I calculate cost per serving for meal prep?

Add up the total ingredient cost across your entire meal prep session, then divide by the total number of meals or servings produced. For a detailed breakdown by recipe, see our guide on how to calculate meal prep cost for a week.

Is this a price per serving calculator or a cost per portion calculator?

Same thing — price per serving, cost per portion, and cost per serving all describe the same math: total cost divided by number of servings. This tool works for any of those framings, whether you're pricing out a single dish, a packaged product, or a restaurant portion to compare against home cooking.
